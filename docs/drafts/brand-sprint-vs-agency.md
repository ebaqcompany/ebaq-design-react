## What each option actually is

### Traditional agency

A traditional agency packages strategy, creative, account management, and often a junior bench behind a senior lead.

You get process, decks, and capacity for big rollouts.

You also get handoffs, waiting for feedback loops, and a price that reflects the overhead.

On the Ebaq site comparison, that model lands at **$25k–$100k+**, **3–9 months or more**, with **account managers** and **junior designers** in the mix.

## When a Brand Sprint wins

Choose a sprint when **speed and senior judgment are the product**.

You are pre-seed to Series B (or a boutique firm) and need an investor-ready or launch-ready identity without waiting a quarter.

You already know roughly who you serve — or you can lock positioning in the first conversations — and you need the look to catch up.

Marketplace or junior freelancers already burned months; you want one accountable senior, not another round of guessing.

Your site or pitch looks like every peer in the category — the problem I unpack in [Why Every AI Startup Looks the Same](/blog/why-ai-startups-look-the-same) — and you need distinctive cues now, not after a long research theater.

**Decision rule:** if a clear identity system in about a week would unblock sales, hiring, or fundraising, the sprint is usually the sharper tool.

## When a traditional agency still makes sense

Agencies earn their keep on **scale and coordination**, not on every logo.

You have many stakeholders, regions, or product lines that need parallel workstreams and a dedicated account layer.

The scope is packaging, retail, vehicles, events, and global guidelines — not just a startup identity and site.

Procurement or board process requires a multi-person team on paper.

You want a long retainer with specialists (media, PR, performance) under one roof, and branding is only one workstream.

**Decision rule:** if the hard problem is coordinating a large organization — not getting to a sharp identity fast — an agency can still be the right buy.

Just compare scopes, not logos on a moodboard.

Ask what is in and out: identity only, messaging, website design, implementation, and rollout.

## When neither is right

This is the answer teams under-use.

A new identity will not fix a broken offer, a missing sales motion, or a product that is not ready to show.

If leadership cannot state who you are for in one sentence, spend on positioning clarity first — not on a logo bake-off.

If the brand is fine and the website is the real drag, scope a site project instead of a full agency rebrand.

If you are still deciding whether to rebrand, refresh, or wait, use the framework in [Should You Rebrand?](/blog/should-i-rebrand) before you book anyone.

**Decision rule:** if the constraint is not the brand, do not buy branding theater to soothe anxiety.

## Decision framework (one page)

Ask these in order.

**1. Is the constraint actually the brand?**

If no → neither. Fix sales, product, or timing first.

**2. Do you need a large bench and multi-quarter coordination?**

If yes → agency (or a scoped retainer that explicitly includes that bench).

**3. Do you need a distinctive, usable identity system in about a week, with senior access?**

If yes → Brand Sprint.

**4. Are you still unsure rebrand vs refresh vs wait?**

Pause hiring. Read [Should You Rebrand?](/blog/should-i-rebrand), then book a short call to pressure-test scope.

**5. Does your category already look identical?**

If yes — especially AI/SaaS — treat distinctiveness as a go-to-market risk, not a cosmetic upgrade ([Why AI startups look the same](/blog/why-ai-startups-look-the-same)).

## FAQ

### Is a Brand Sprint "cheap branding"?

No.

Cheap is marketplace roulette and junior guesswork.

A sprint is **focused senior work** at a clear price — not an agency overhead stack.

### Can a sprint replace a full agency AOR?

Not if you need media buying, PR, and a ten-person team.

Yes if your near-term need is identity (and optionally a site) done with judgment and speed.

### What if we need a website too?

Identity first when the look is broken.

Then scope the site — Framer, Webflow, or React — as a follow-on, not as a surprise add-on mid-sprint.

### What if we only have a few weeks before a raise or launch?

That is exactly when agency timelines of **3–9 months** fail founders.

A one-week core identity is built for that window — if you can decide quickly.
