What that means:

- The head terms `branding` and `what is branding` are owned by HubSpot, AMA, Wikipedia, Investopedia, Vistaprint, Indeed and Merriam-Webster. A history article will not rank for them on its own, and a third page chasing "what is branding" would make the existing cannibalization worse.
- This article therefore targets **history of branding** and its family (evolution of branding, when did branding start, who invented branding, origin of the word brand, first registered trademark, oldest brand), where the ranking pages are Vistaprint, Skyword, a college blog, an agency blog and a university news post. That is a winnable SERP.
- It carries a short "What is branding?" definition section near the top for snippet eligibility, and links with exact-match anchors to `/blog/what-is-branding`, `/blog/what-is-a-brand` and `/blog/branding-brand-identity`, so it feeds authority to the definition page instead of competing with it.
- It is built as a linkable asset (original framework, sourced myth corrections, a data chart, a timeline table), which is how a site this size earns the links the head terms require.

## Myths and corrections applied

Each of these is commonly repeated on the ranking pages and is handled more carefully in the draft.

1. **"Egyptian tomb paintings from 2700 BCE"**: weakly sourced (Wikipedia cites a management journal). Solid evidence is New Kingdom tomb art, such as the Met's Nebamun facsimile; the draft says so explicitly.
2. **Etymology**: not simply "Old Norse brandr, to burn". Old English brand/brond, fire or torch; verb c. 1400; mark on casks 1550s; "a particular make of goods" 1854; brand-name 1889; brand loyalty 1961 (Etymonline).
3. **1266 bakers' marks**: the Assize of Bread and Ale date is traditional and the marking clause is not in the statute text most sources show; attributed to WIPO as "often cited".
4. **Stella Artois 1366**: a tax record for the Den Hoorn brewery; Artois family 1717, Stella brand 1926. Löwenbräu 1383 has been debunked and is left out.
5. **Bass**: UK trade mark No. 1 is the pale ale label containing the red triangle, not the bare triangle, and it is still in force; the overnight queue story is "reportedly", per the UK IPO itself.
6. **J. Walter Thompson**: bought the agency in 1877; Red and Blue Books date from 1899 to 1904, not 1889.
7. **Uneeda Biscuit**: 1898 to 1899, not 1896; "first million-dollar campaign" is a label, worded as such.
8. **Brand manuals**: NASA (manual issued January 1976) and British Rail (1965) were not the first; Lufthansa 1963 is listed too.
9. **Kraft**: the deal was about $13.1 billion ($12.9 billion in much of the literature); worded "about $13 billion". Rowntree's "five times book value" could not be verified, so the draft uses "more than double its pre-bid market value".
10. **Marlboro Friday**: shares fell 23 percent (Washington Post arithmetic), about $10 billion; Wikipedia's 26 percent is not used.
11. **Interbrand**: first brand valuation 1988, but the Best Global Brands ranking started in 2000.
12. **Quotes**: Landor's "created in the mind" line has no primary source, so it is "attributed to"; the Bezos "not in the room" line is not used.
13. **Scaurus mosaic**: it was in his own atrium, so it is self-promotion rather than public advertising.
14. **Rankings**: Interbrand 2025 has Apple first at $470.9 billion; Kantar BrandZ 2026 has Google first at about $1.485 trillion. Ocean Tomo: intangibles 17 percent of S&P 500 value in 1975, about 92 percent in the 2025 study.

Link check: all 22 external links return 200 except five that block bots but open in a browser (Britannica, Ocean Tomo, Fast Company x2, Taylor & Francis) and the Met (rate limited). A dead Kellogg School link for the 1955 HBR article was removed.
